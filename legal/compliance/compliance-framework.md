# Compliance Framework

**Version:** 1.0
**Effective Date:** 2024-01-01
**Last Reviewed:** 2024-01-01

---

## 1. Introduction

This Compliance Framework defines the Marketplace's approach to regulatory and legal compliance across all jurisdictions in which it operates. It establishes the structure, responsibilities, and processes for identifying, monitoring, and meeting compliance obligations.

---

## 2. Compliance Objectives

The Marketplace's compliance programme aims to:

- Ensure the platform operates lawfully in every market it enters
- Protect users from harm through enforceable policies and procedures
- Manage legal and regulatory risk proactively
- Maintain trust with users, regulators, partners, and investors
- Enable scalable growth by embedding compliance into product and operations

---

## 3. Compliance Governance

### 3.1 Legal and Compliance Team

The Legal and Compliance Team is responsible for:

- Identifying applicable laws and regulations in each operating jurisdiction
- Developing and maintaining compliance policies
- Advising the business on regulatory requirements
- Managing regulatory relationships and enquiries
- Conducting internal compliance assessments

### 3.2 Business Unit Compliance

Each business unit is responsible for:

- Implementing compliance requirements within its operations
- Identifying compliance risks in its processes
- Escalating compliance concerns to the Legal Team
- Completing required compliance training

### 3.3 Board-Level Oversight

The Board of Directors (or equivalent governance body) is responsible for:

- Approving the compliance programme and significant policy changes
- Receiving regular compliance reports
- Ensuring adequate resources are allocated to compliance

---

## 4. Regulatory Scope

### 4.1 Current Operating Jurisdictions

The Marketplace tracks regulatory requirements in all jurisdictions where it operates or intends to operate. Current priority jurisdictions include:

| Region | Key Regulatory Areas |
|---|---|
| European Union | GDPR, Consumer Rights Directive, Digital Services Act, AML/CFT |
| United Kingdom | UK GDPR, Consumer Contracts Regulations, Online Safety Act |
| Nigeria | NDPA, Consumer Protection Act, FCCPC, CBN payment regulations |
| Other markets | As assessed on market entry |

### 4.2 Regulatory Categories

The compliance programme covers:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Data protection and privacy | GDPR, NDPA, CCPA |
| Consumer protection | Cancellation rights, refund obligations, fairness rules |
| Financial services / payments | Payment services regulation, AML/KYC |
| Employment and contractor law | Worker classification, tax obligations |
| Anti-money laundering (AML) | KYC requirements, suspicious activity reporting |
| Anti-bribery and corruption | Bribery Act, FCPA |
| Intellectual property | Copyright, trademark, platform liability |
| Platform regulation | Digital Services Act, Online Safety Act |
| Tax | VAT/GST obligations, marketplace tax facilitation |
| Industry-specific | Regulated professions (healthcare, legal, financial advice) |

## 5. Compliance Risk Assessment

### 5.1 Annual Risk Assessment

The Legal Team conducts an annual compliance risk assessment that:

- Identifies applicable legal and regulatory obligations
- Assesses the likelihood and impact of non-compliance
- Prioritises risks for remediation
- Maps risks to control owners
- Tracks remediation progress

### 5.2 Ad Hoc Assessments

Compliance assessments are also triggered by:

- Entry into a new jurisdiction
- Launch of a new product or service
- Material change in regulatory landscape
- Acquisition or partnership
- Significant incident or complaint

### 5.3 Risk Register

The Legal Team maintains a Compliance Risk Register documenting all identified risks, associated controls, ownership, and status.

## 6. Policies and Controls

### 6.1 Policy Hierarchy

| Level | Document Type | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Core policies | Privacy Policy, Terms of Service |
| 2 | Supporting policies | Data Processing Policy, Dispute Policy |
| 3 | Procedures | Data breach procedure, KYC procedure |
| 4 | Guidelines and templates | Legal notice templates, consent templates |

### 6.2 Policy Review Cycle

| Policy Type | Review Frequency |
|---|---|
| Core policies | Annually or upon material regulatory change |
| Supporting policies | Annually |
| Procedures | As needed; minimum annually |

### 6.3 Policy Approval

All new or materially changed policies require:

1. Drafting by the Legal Team
2. Review by relevant business stakeholders
3. DPO review (for data-related policies)
4. Legal sign-off
5. Board/Senior Management approval (for core policies)
6. Publication and communication to affected parties

---

## 7. Training and Awareness

All employees receive:

- Data protection training at onboarding and annually
- Anti-bribery and corruption training at onboarding and bi-annually
- Role-specific compliance training (e.g., AML training for finance roles)

Training completion is tracked by the People Team. Non-completion triggers a follow-up process.

---

## 8. Third-Party Compliance

The Marketplace's compliance obligations extend to how third parties handle data and conduct business on its behalf. Requirements:

- All vendors and processors undergo compliance due diligence before engagement
- Data processing agreements are executed with all processors
- Key partner agreements include compliance warranties and audit rights
- Significant third-party compliance failures trigger immediate review

---

## 9. Regulatory Engagement

The Marketplace proactively engages with regulators by:

- Responding to all regulatory enquiries promptly and accurately
- Participating in regulatory consultations where relevant
- Monitoring regulatory developments and adapting policies proactively
- Appointing designated contacts for key regulatory relationships

---

## 10. Compliance Monitoring and Reporting

### 10.1 Internal Monitoring

The Legal Team conducts quarterly internal compliance monitoring, reviewing:

- Policy adherence across business units
- Open compliance issues and remediation progress
- Training completion rates
- Regulatory correspondence and actions

### 10.2 Compliance Reporting

A quarterly compliance report is submitted to senior management covering:

- Regulatory developments
- Compliance incidents and near-misses
- Open risk items
- Policy updates

---

## 11. Incident Management

Compliance incidents (including regulatory breaches, significant policy violations, or regulatory investigations) are:

1. Reported to the Legal Team immediately
2. Assessed for severity and regulatory notification obligation
3. Documented in the Compliance Incident Register
4. Remediated with root cause analysis
